# 00 — Brief, Strategy and Working Principles

> **Status:** exploration. Nothing in this folder is final. **No logo, palette, typeface or material system is selected** until a direction is approved (see `04-critique-and-recommendation.md`).
> **Production environment:** Claude Code only. Earlier Codex-oriented material, and the earlier *Warp & Weft* direction for "Pixelard", are archived in `docs/archive/2026-10-warp-and-weft/` and superseded by this brief.

## 1. The brief

**Pixelards** is an Australian, **AI-first creative studio**.
- **Branding-first**, with major capabilities in:
  - content distribution, social growth, SEO and AI search;
  - performance advertising and creative production;
  - websites, automation and e-commerce growth;
  - marketing strategy and video marketing.
- **Concept: THE LIVING SIGNAL.** It combines two ideas:
  1. **A Living Identity:** a brand is a flexible, evolving visual system, not a static logo.
  2. **A Distribution Machine:** that identity becomes a connected universe of content, campaigns, experiences and communications distributed across channels.
- **The website must express this philosophy through its own visual system and interactions.** It needs an original identity that transforms, multiplies, adapts, travels and reconnects through the experience, and stays recognisable throughout.
- **Ambition reference:** lusion.co, for execution quality only. Never copy its identity, assets, layout or interactions.
- **First assignment:** three exceptional, genuinely different visual executions of The Living Signal, then a recommendation. **Wait for approval** before finalising the identity or building.

## 2. Strategic reading

**What Pixelards really sells.** Most studios sell *either* the brand (identity agencies) *or* the reach (growth and performance shops). Pixelards sells the **continuity between them**: an identity designed from day one to be *distributed*. It is built to be cut down, re-formatted, localised, sequenced, searched and A/B-tested without losing itself. The site has to make that continuity **visible and felt**.

**The core design question** behind all three executions:
> *What stays constant, so the brand is recognisable, while everything else changes, so it can live everywhere?*

Every execution must answer it with an explicit **genome**, made of two parts:
- **Invariants:** the few things that never change.
- **Variables:** the many things that adapt to channel, format, audience, time and data.

**Visibility of the machine.**
- The Distribution Machine must be shown **as craft, not as a dashboard.** No analytics-chart clichés.
- It must be honest: the site can show how a signal *propagates* across formats without inventing results, reach numbers or clients.

**AI-first, without the clichés.**
- AI shows up as an **operating philosophy**: systems over one-offs, rules that generate families of outputs, speed of iteration, versioning, testing, learning loops.
- It shows up **in the behaviour of the identity**, never as imagery. No glowing brains, neural-net graphs, robot hands, sparkle icons, purple-blue "AI gradients" or "generated by AI" aesthetics.

**Australian, without the clichés.**
- No kangaroos, landmarks, flags or Southern Cross.
- **Never** use or imitate Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander art, symbols or songlines (cultural appropriation).
- If Australia appears at all, it is through *quality of light, distance, time zone* (the day starts here) or *verified* history. Any historical claim must be checked before use.

**Naming context (verified 2026-10-08).** There is no existing public presence for "Pixelards". Several Australian studios share the "Pixel" stem (Pixel Zoo, Pixel in Melbourne, Pixel Factory in Brisbane).
- **Implication:** the identity must **not** lean on pixel-square or 8-bit imagery, or it will blend into that crowd.
- The name's distinctiveness lives in the **"-ards"** ending and the plural (*many* outputs from one source). That is a useful seed: **one studio, many signals.**

## 3. What we take from the reference (principles, not identity)
From `docs/archive/2026-10-warp-and-weft/01-reference-analysis.md` (homepage and About studied in depth, including the shipped code):

| # | Principle | Meaning for Pixelards |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | **3-second test** | Within 3 s a visitor sees craft, acts and gets a response, and reads one plain sentence about what we do |
| H2 | **The hero toy *is* the idea** | Interacting with the hero should *demonstrate* the Living Signal (an identity that adapts and travels), not just show rendering skill |
| H3 | **Type beside the world** | The statement has its own clear space; the 3D stage has a frame that doubles as a transition device |
| H4 | **The preloader hands off through the brand** | What you watch while loading becomes the first scene |
| H5 | **A repeatable reward** | A second simple input produces a new variation (the identity *varying* is the reward) |
| H6 | **Play leaves a trace** | What the visitor makes persists and pays off later (e.g. a visitor-generated variant travels through the site) |
| P1–P10 | System rules | One persistent world; DOM-first; scroll as a reversible timeline; authored cameras; spring physics for input; input changes a *world property*; designed sound; content leads; performance as a design constraint; no dead ends |

- the rounded dark hero card on an off-white page;

- the tunnels and astronauts;

## 4. Standards (the bar)
- **Taste and restraint.** Every element has a reason. Fewer, better moves.
- **Originality.** If a visual idea can be traced to a famous precedent, we either transform it until it is ours or drop it. Each execution includes a **derivation audit**.
- **Avoid:**
  - generic agency templates and SaaS layouts;
  - stock-looking 3D (chrome blobs, glossy abstract shapes);
  - excessive glassmorphism;
  - meaningless particles;
  - generic AI imagery;
  - globe-with-arcs or connected-dots "network" visuals for distribution;
  - inflated copy;
  - motion without purpose.
- **Honesty.** No fabricated case studies, client results, awards, testimonials or credentials. Placeholders are labelled.
- **Production quality.** Performance, accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA), mobile usability and maintainability are design inputs, not afterthoughts.

## 5. How the executions are judged
| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| **Originality** | Would a jury recognise it as new? What does the derivation audit find? |
| **Visual quality** | Does a single keyframe stop you? Is there restraint and craft? |
| **Positioning** | Does it express *branding-first* **and** *distribution* **and** *AI-first operating philosophy* without saying so in clichés? |
| **The genome** | Are the invariants strong enough to stay recognisable across extreme variation? |
| **Distribution made visible** | Does the site *show* one signal becoming many formats and channels, as craft? |
| **Hero (H1–H6)** | 3-second test; is the toy the thesis? |
| **Technical feasibility** | Can it ship at 60 fps desktop / 30 fps mid-range mobile, with a DOM-first fallback? |
| **Long-term brand potential** | Does the system generate a decade of social posts, decks, campaigns, merch, motion, OOH, without the studio redrawing it each time? |

## 6. Process
1. **Diverge:** five independent creative lenses each proposed two executions (ten candidates).
2. **Critique:** two independent critics (a brand/creative lens and a derivation and technical lens) attacked every candidate.
3. **Select:** three executions chosen for strength *and* mutual difference.
4. **Develop:** each execution was specified in depth, then cross-critiqued by a different reviewer and revised.
5. **Visualise:** homepage keyframes and identity studies rendered as real WebGL + DOM compositions, inspected and iterated.
6. **Recommend:** a scored recommendation, plus what must be resolved before production.

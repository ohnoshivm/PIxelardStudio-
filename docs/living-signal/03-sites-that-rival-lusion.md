# 03 · Sites that rival Lusion

**Purpose.** These are the studio's reference set for the reset after Direction A was rejected. They are sites at Lusion's level of craft, each with what to take from it and what to avoid.

**How to use it.**
- Open every link on a real GPU (desktop Chrome or Safari, ideally with sound on). Most of these sites cannot be judged from a still: they are motion, sound and response to the hand.
- Third-party screenshots are not committed to this repo. A contact sheet of each site's own share image was sent to the studio separately.

**Sourcing.** The list was compiled by web research on 9 Oct 2026, starting from 33 candidates. Each entry links to its award listing or case study so the studio can check the claims.

Awwwards blocks requests from this build environment, so award details were not re-checked from here. Liveness was checked by loading each URL.

Nothing here is copied into Pixelards. The non-negotiables in `00` still apply, including the Lusion no-copy list.

## What each one does, and what to take

### 1. Igloo Inc
- **Signature.**
  - Each portfolio company is its own block of ice, grown by a procedural crystal algorithm.
  - One scroll carries the camera through a single frozen world.
  - Transitions use frost, displacement and chromatic aberration, rendered live rather than played as video.
- **Take.**
  - Use one material system with endless variation: the set reads as one world, yet no two objects are alike.
  - Make art and code together in the browser, with live tuning tools.
- **Avoid.** Every piece of text is drawn in WebGL and the HTML body is empty. That fails a DOM-first, WCAG 2.2 AA build.

### 2. Messenger
- **Signature.**
  - A tiny walkable planet, shared live by up to 10 players.
  - Every colour in the world comes from one 16×16 texture.
- **Take.**
  - Drive the whole palette from one small token source, so a mood change is a single edit.
  - Budget mobile memory explicitly.
  - Its performance score (8.8 WPO) shows ambition and speed can coexist.

### 3. Immersive Garden
- **Signature.**
  - A sculpted bas-relief of natural forms is the studio's calling card.
  - 3D Roman numerals act as section anchors.
  - A hidden "Backstage" section shows how the work is made.
- **Take.**
  - One restrained, tactile material can carry the voice while client work stays the hero.
  - The team says it spent more time removing elements than adding them.
  - Copy the pipeline: KTX texture compression, channel-packed textures and automated glTF exports.
- **Avoid.** Its viewport blocks pinch-zoom (`user-scalable=0`).

### 4. Active Theory V6
- **Signature.**
  - A navigable real-time 3D portfolio, released like a software version.
  - It includes an AI chat and multiplayer presence.
- **Take.**
  - Treat the studio site as a versioned product: each relaunch should be a real technical step, not a reskin.
  - If the site uses AI, make it a useful guide to the work, never an AI visual motif.
- **Avoid.** Near-empty HTML, and zoom is blocked.

### 5. Bruno Simon (2025)
- **Signature.**
  - A drivable physics world built in three.js with TSL, on WebGPU where available.
  - Every visitor shares the same weather and time of day.
  - The menus are objects you drive into.
- **Take.**
  - Use shared real-world state as a reason to come back, without full multiplayer.
  - Ship a lower-quality mobile preset from day one.
  - The front-end code is MIT-licensed, so it can be studied.
- **Avoid.** UI built as 3D models; keep navigation in HTML.

### 6. GQ × Audemars Piguet: The Extraordinary Lab
- **Signature.**
  - One continuous cinematic timeline that visitors scrub like a media player.
  - Camera, dialogue, environment and sound are all bound to it.
  - It targets 60 fps on an iPhone 11.
- **Take.**
  - Bind every layer to one master timeline, so scrolling in either direction lands on a coherent state.
  - Reduced motion then becomes stepping through the same keyframes without the tweening.
  - It shows Lusion-grade craft can live inside a host platform's page.

### 7. Lando Norris
- **Signature.**
  - One lime accent on near-black.
  - A WebGL helmet hero, Rive motion and fast transitions, built on Webflow.
- **Take.** **This is the architecture to copy.**
  - It serves real HTML content and navigation before any JavaScript runs, with WebGL as an enhancement layer.
  - It won Site of the Year with one 3D centrepiece, one bold colour and consistent motion rules.

### 8. Cartier Watches & Wonders 2025
- **Signature.**
  - Six self-contained 3D alcoves, each built around one watch.
  - Hidden gestures, and a Web Audio soundscape treated as narrative.
- **Take.**
  - Break a long story into a few rooms with one shared grammar.
  - Make sound opt-in and narrative.
  - Build the experience as an embeddable component that mounts into a host page.
- **Note.** The live URL is the development host that Awwwards links to. The campaign itself ran on cartier.com.

### 9. Unseen Studio
- **Signature.**
  - A surreal pink 3D "home" where each page is its own scene.
  - Navigation is a physical camera journey, including a dive underwater to reach the work.
- **Take.**
  - Join pages through space rather than cuts.
  - Keep real DOM navigation over the WebGL.
  - Offer "enter without audio" at the door.
- **Avoid.** Zoom is blocked.

### 10. The Symphony of Vines
- **Signature.**
  - A wine estate's terroir told as interactive geology.
  - The visitor's cursor forms rivers, soil and waves.
- **Take.** Find the one process that made the brand, and let the visitor perform it with their own hand rather than watch an illustration of it.

### 11. Shopify Editions Winter '26
- **Signature.**
  - More than 150 product updates presented as a scroll gallery of 3D Renaissance-style paintings.
  - AI generated each painting's foundations, and human artists finished depth, light and animation.
- **Take.**
  - It is the most DOM-first piece in the set: about 5,300 words of real HTML, with a skip link.
  - AI is used as a disclosed production step that humans finish. **That is an honest AI-first position Pixelards can own.**

### 12. Santioni Spirits: Notturno
- **Signature.**
  - A drinks brand told as a living comic.
  - A custom ink-brush shader gives every panel grain and bleeding edges.
  - "Hold and move" turns each frame into a stage.
- **Take.**
  - Apply strict art direction at the shader level: one treatment, one hero colour and one reserved accent.
  - This makes 3D feel authored rather than generic.

### 13. Persepolis Reimagined
- **Signature.** A real-time reconstruction of ancient Persepolis, toured by a guided narrative camera.
- **Take.**
  - A chaptered camera path keeps heavy 3D usable for a general audience.
  - It allows zoom up to 5×, which proves award-level WebGL does not need to block zoom.

### 14. Ponpon Mania
- **Signature.** A two-person interactive comic: the camera moves panel to panel, with physics-driven play.
- **Take.** Authorship beats polygon count. It gets Lusion-like play from illustration and small physical interactions on a small budget, and scores 8.2 for performance.
- **Avoid.** Zoom is blocked.

### 15. Why Zero
- **Signature.** A sound-led, single-page 3D narrative for an AI-native university, with **no AI visual clichés**.
- **Take.** It is the closest analogue to Pixelards' own position. Match its tone, and beat it on accessibility: it blocks zoom and scores 6.6 for accessibility.

## The patterns that make these Lusion-level

1. **One world, one material grammar.** Examples: Igloo's ice, Santioni's ink, Immersive Garden's relief, Messenger's 16×16 palette.
   - The whole site obeys one physical rule set.
   - Variation comes from inside that rule set, never from mixing styles.
2. **Navigation is a journey, not a page change.** Examples: Unseen's dive, Igloo's single scroll, Bruno's drive, GQ's timeline.
   - Moving through the site is itself the brand story.
3. **The visitor causes it.** Examples: Symphony of Vines, Santioni's hold and move, Bruno's car.
   - The best moments happen because of the visitor's hand, not in front of it.
4. **Sound is a narrative layer, and it is opt-in.** Examples: Unseen, Cartier, GQ, Why Zero.
5. **Authored art direction beats polygon count.** Examples: Ponpon Mania, Santioni, Shopify.
   - A distinctive hand reads as premium; generic realism does not.
6. **The winners that ship real HTML prove DOM-first is not a compromise.** Examples: Lando Norris, Shopify Editions.
   - Most of the others block zoom or ship empty HTML.
   - **Pixelards can match the craft and beat the whole set on access.**

## Considered and left out

These were checked and dropped, with the reason for each:

- **Not live:** Opal Tadpole.
- **Could not be verified:** UNESCO Virtual Museum.
- **Lighter 3D craft than the top 15:** Noomo, Noomo Labs, basement.studio (2025), Phantom.Land, Akaru, Hubtown, Aether 1, Explore Primland.
- **Not real-time 3D:** Lama Lama, Locomotive, Exo Ape, The Line Studio, AW Portfolio, David Whyte Experience.
- **Dated site:** Resn's own site, from 2016. Its 2025 client work is the better reference.

**Kept outside the 3D set:** Locomotive remains a DOM-first accessibility and performance benchmark.
